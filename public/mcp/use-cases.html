ThriveStack MCP

Use Cases

Example prompts and workflows for the ThriveStack MCP Server. Browse by type if you need ideas on what to ask.

Start by just asking a question — your AI assistant resolves your product and environment automatically from how you connected, so there’s no project to pick first:

“Is our ThriveStack setup finished, and what’s still pending?”
Include a time range in your questions. “Last 30 days”, “this month”, or “past week” gets you better results.
For a running growth-advisor conversation instead of one-off questions, invoke the growth_advisor prompt (see Prompts).

Example workflows

1. Growth health check

“Why is activation dropping this month?”

The ask tool routes this to explain_activation (and pulls in signup/feature-usage context as needed), returning funnel drop-off points, time to each milestone, and which accounts are stuck at which stage.

2. MRR and churn trend

“What’s our MRR trend over the last 6 months, and which accounts churned last month?”

Combines get_mrr_trend with get_churn_accounts/explain_churn — MoM MRR trend plus churned accounts classified as hard churn, scheduled, or already lapsed, with LTV and last payment date for each.

3. Monthly PLG scorecard

“Give me a scorecard of our funnel over the last year — where were we weakest?”

get_monthly_metrics_table returns a 12-month, color-coded scorecard across visitors, signups, time-to-signup, new paying accounts, and new MRR, so weak months stand out immediately.

4. Revenue attribution

“Which marketing channel drives the most revenue, and what’s the LTV per channel?”

get_channel_revenue_attribution (step=channels) ranks channels by revenue with an LTV proxy; follow up with step=breakdown for one channel’s campaigns, sources, and customers.

5. AI visibility gap check

“Where are we losing AI citations for ourdomain.com, and what should we publish to fix it?”

get_domain_report and get_citation_probe_data surface the visibility report and raw citation-gap evidence; get_owned_content_recommendations turns the gaps into ranked content recommendations, and generate_content_outline drafts a structure for any one of them.

Real-world workflows

Diagnose a bad month end-to-end

“Something felt off in October — walk me through what happened to signups, MRR, and activation.”

The assistant chains get_signup_trend, get_mrr_trend/get_mrr_waterfall, and explain_activation for the month, synthesizing a single narrative instead of three disconnected numbers — this is exactly the kind of multi-source question ask is designed to route on its own.

Turn an AI-visibility gap into a content plan

“What content should we publish this week to close our biggest AI citation gaps?”

get_owned_content_recommendations returns gap-scored, prioritized recommendations with the evidence behind each; pick the top one and call generate_content_outline to get headings, an answer-first summary, FAQ questions, and schema markup suggestions to hand to a writer.

Instrument a new repo from scratch

“Help me add ThriveStack tracking to this marketing site.”

The assistant asks whether you need marketing, product, or both, and for your API key, then calls start_thrivestack_setup and works through the returned wizard prompts one at a time — or you can invoke thrivestack_marketing_setup_wizard / thrivestack_product_setup_wizard directly (see Prompts).

Tips

To make the experience smoother or more complete, take a look at the following tips:

  • Include dates: Always specify a time range or month (e.g. “last 7 days”, “October 2026”).
  • Name accounts: Mention a specific account by ID or name when you want a drill-down instead of a portfolio view.
  • Let discover_product_schema run first: For anything referencing your own event or feature names, the assistant needs to discover your real event taxonomy before it can query it correctly — don’t worry, it does this automatically.
  • Start broad, then drill down: Ask for the overview first, then follow up on whatever stands out.
